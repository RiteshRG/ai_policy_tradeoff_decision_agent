Lens Question: How does India's E20 ethanol‑blending programme affect vehicle performance and consumer costs?

Key Findings
- Newer, E20‑compatible cars (manufactured post‑2017) typically see a modest 2‑4% reduction in mileage, reflecting ethanol’s lower energy density (Reuters, 2025).
- Older gasoline engines—especially pre‑2015 models and two‑stroke motorcycles—experience larger fuel‑economy losses (5‑10%) and may develop corrosion in fuel‑system components not designed for ethanol (Team‑BHP, 2024).
- Flex‑fuel vehicles (FFVs) and models with updated fuel‑system seals incur no measurable engine‑wear issues, while non‑compatible vehicles risk fuel‑pump seal degradation and carburetor clogging (Milexglobal, 2026).
- Ethanol is cheaper per litre than pure petrol, but its lower energy content can offset price savings, yielding near‑neutral net spend for most drivers (Milexglobal, 2026).
- Vehicle‑class differences are pronounced: passenger cars see modest mileage loss, whereas two‑wheelers and small‑engine commercial vans report higher sensitivity to E20 due to older carbureted designs.

Positive Impacts
- Supports a transition to cleaner‑burning fuel, reducing tail‑pipe emissions for compatible vehicles without major hardware changes.
- Provides a domestic fuel source that can lower wholesale petrol prices, benefitting owners of newer, E20‑ready cars.
- Encourages automakers to adopt ethanol‑compatible engine designs, future‑proofing the fleet.

Negative Impacts
- Older vehicles may suffer reduced fuel‑economy and accelerated wear of fuel‑system seals, leading to higher maintenance costs.
- Two‑stroke motorcycles and legacy commercial fleets risk engine damage, prompting premature part replacement or retrofitting expenses.
- Inconsistent compatibility across vehicle classes creates consumer confusion and potential fuel‑misuse at pumps.

Lens Assessment: E20 blending delivers modest environmental gains but imposes measurable performance and cost penalties on older and non‑compatible vehicles, creating a split impact that hinges on fleet age and engine design.
